Walk Jezero

You start where Perseverance landed. The ground around you is NASA's HiRISE elevation model (about 1 m grid: 1.0 m north–south, 0.95 m east–west) with the 0.3 m/px HiRISE orbital photo; beyond about 1 km it continues into coarser measured terrain up to the crater rim and the horizon. Walk the roughly 2 × 2 km area (2.05 × 1.94 km) with the helmet HUD. The reference route to Rochette is a MartisDux planner output, not safety-certified.

About AR on phones

Android Chrome (ARCore) uses your real movement: walk and turn to look around, or hold Hold to walk to move further. Phone AR starts with the camera visible above the terrain. Mars sky fills the screen with the simulation; keep to a clear space and watch where you step. Room shows the camera image above the Martian horizon. Devices without immersive WebXR can use the first-person preview. Headsets with WebXR VR get full immersion.

Terrain data

A reconstruction from archived orbital data, not a live view of Mars. Heights are shown ×2 by default so slopes and ridges read at eye level (not to scale); untick Relief ×2 for true scale. The ground lies on the Mars sphere (radius 3,396,190 m, planetocentric latitude, east longitude), so the horizon curves away as it would there. HiRISE and CTX images are monochrome; they are shown with one uniform hue, the average Viking colour of Mars. The sun is a fixed display light, not today's Sun. Ground shape and height come from the measured DEM. The HUD uses unexaggerated slopes and cannot assess radiation, dust, rocks, or soil strength. The only generated content is the optional illustrative detail within 30 m of you: fine regolith texture and small rock clusters (placed where the DEM is steeper or the HiRISE image brighter). It is not a NASA observation, may not be to scale, and does not change the ground height you walk on; beyond 30 m everything is measured data. Nothing is sharpened, upscaled, AI-generated or derived from image brightness; where finer data ends, the view continues on coarser real data.
